Self-introduction.

Fourth-year Psychology student at the University of Edinburgh, specialising in psycholinguistics and experimental research methods. My research explores how people comprehend non-literal language (particularly sarcasm) across spoken and written communication.

Beyond the lab I am active in student engagement and care about open science and science communication, including writing psychology articles for general audiences.

Currently preparing to pursue graduate study in psycholinguistics, with interests spanning experimental pragmatics and cognitive approaches to language comprehension.

02 — Current Research

2025 → 2026

How capitalisation affects sarcasm comprehension in online communication

Dissertation (Grade: 85) · Supervised by Prof. Martin Corley

Independent dissertation on whether capitalisation helps sarcasm sarcasm comprehension. Designed and implemented an online experiment using PCIbex (JavaScript), collected 113 participants; analysis suggests capitalisation functions as a pragmatic salience marker rather than a sarcasm-specific cue. Talk presented on BPS Scotland Undergraduate Conference 2026 at the University of Strathclyde Glasgow.

Writing up
2025 →

Parafoveal-on-foveal effects of emoji in sarcasm comprehension during Chinese reading

Carnegie Trust Vacation Scholarship · Dr. Patrick Sturt

Pre-registered independent study examining how emoji previewed in parafoveal vision shape sarcasm interpretation in Chinese text. Designed and programmed the eye-tracking experiment using EyeLink 1000 and Experiment Builder, collected 66 Chinese adults, analysed eye-tracking data. Results suggests that emoji–word semantic congruency influences late re-reading but not initial processing, showing a different pattern from previously reported effects of object emojis.

2025 →

How conversational context shifts language prediction

With Dr Tianyue Wang, Dr Wei Li · Supervised by Prof. Martin Corley and Prof. Hannah Rhode

Visual-world eye-tracking experiment testing whether explicitly communicative settings pull predictions away from typicality and toward informativity. OpenSesame; data collection ongoing.
